1873

A house built to be lived in

The Todd House was raised in 1873 in the French Second Empire style, and restored — room by room, object by object — over 37 years. What you find inside is not a museum. The piano plays, the fireplaces are lit, and the ping-pong table is in constant use.

✦

One

Parlor & Library

The heart of the house. A marble gold-inlaid fireplace and a tall secretary anchor the room; bay windows and a chandelier do the rest. Settle into the antique furniture and take in what's on display — the antiquarian library, a stereoscope, marble-top tables and a good deal else besides.

It is the room guests drift back to after dinner, and the one they're still sitting in when we say goodnight.

Two

The Music Room

Exquisite Victorian furnishings, intricate crown molding and a fireplace that makes the room in winter. A player piano, a pump organ and a victrola stand ready for anyone who wants to try them — and most people do, eventually.

Step away from the noise of the week and let the house play you something instead.

Three

The Dining Room

Breakfast is served here, at a table laid with formal china, beneath an elegant chandelier and beside a warm fireplace. An ornate sideboard runs along one wall.

The room seats ten, which makes it equally right for a quiet breakfast for two or a celebration that takes over the whole table.

Four

The Game Room

A recent addition, and an unexpectedly popular one. Ping pong, stand-up shuffleboard, a pool table, air hockey and a small model train board — with antiques arranged around the room and across the ceiling.

It has been very well received by our guests, which is a polite way of saying the tournaments run late.

Five

The Courtyard

A peaceful oasis behind the house: ivy, ferns and flowers, a hand-crafted brick patio, and five round tables seating four apiece, with table umbrellas for shade and extra chairs and side tables scattered about.

It is where the afternoon goes when nobody is watching the clock.

Six

The Front Porch

Victorian rugs, wicker furniture with cushions, rocking chairs, love seats, coffee tables and end tables — a large seating area facing Third Street, and the best place in the house to watch Beaver go by.

For cooler nights there are table-top heaters. The butter churn and crocks are purely for atmosphere, and they earn their keep.

And the rest of it

Rooms worth wandering into

The Butler's Pantry

A collection of Victorian-era curiosities: mustache cups, teapots, chocolate sets, cut-glass pitchers, an oak ice box and a porcelain-top table. Guests are welcome to ask what any of it was for — we know, and we'll tell you.

The Kitchen

Charming, cozy and designed to make you feel at home, with seating for six, Monet-inspired decor, a Keurig and a water cooler. The room where guests end up talking long after breakfast has been cleared.

The Entrance Hall

A testament to the Victorian love of grandeur — an exquisite chandelier, ornate furnishings and a collection of fine Victorian hats and canes that guests are encouraged to try on. Our motto here is too much is not enough.

Gatherings

The house makes a very good occasion

Weddings and getting-ready mornings, baby and bridal showers, tea parties, reunions, club meetings and birthdays — the common rooms have hosted all of them, and the staircase has been in more wedding photographs than we can count.

Tell us what you have in mind and we will tell you honestly whether the house suits it.
